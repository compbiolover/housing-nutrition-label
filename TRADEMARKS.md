## Why this is separate from the code license

Copyright and trademark protect different things. Copyright covers the code; a trademark
covers the *name people use to find the thing*. They also have different reach in time:

- Releases through **v0.1.82** were published under the MIT License and stay MIT
  permanently. That code can be forked and built on by anyone, forever.
- The trademark applies regardless. Someone may lawfully fork the MIT-era code — they may
  **not** call the result "Housing Nutrition Label", and they may not present it as this
  project.

So the mark, not the license, is what keeps a fork from being mistaken for the original.

## What you may do without asking

Ordinary **referential (nominative) use** is fine and always will be. You do not need
permission to:

- Say your project works with, reads from, or is built on Housing Nutrition Label.
- Write about, review, teach, benchmark or criticize the project by name.
- Cite it in academic work, journalism or documentation.
- Link to housinglabel.dev or the GitHub repository.
- Keep the existing attribution notices in a fork, as the license in fact requires.

The test is simple: use the name to **refer to this project**, use no more of it than you
need, and don't imply endorsement.

## What needs written permission

- Naming your fork, product, service, company or domain "Housing Nutrition Label", or
  anything close enough to be confused with it.
- Using the marks in a way that suggests this project sponsors, endorses or is affiliated
  with yours.
- Using the marks as, or as part of, your own brand, logo, app name or social handle.
- Selling merchandise or offering a certification bearing the marks.

## Forks

Forks are welcome within the terms of [LICENSE](LICENSE) — please just give yours its own
name. Something like "Foo, built on Housing Nutrition Label™" is referential use and is
fine; "Housing Nutrition Label Pro" is not.
